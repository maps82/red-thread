# Project Red Thread

> Software remembers data.  
> Humans remember stories.  
> Relationships are remembered through continuity.

## Vision

Large Language Models have become exceptionally good at understanding people.

Yet they still suffer from one fundamental limitation:

They do not truly remember.

A conversation that spans days, weeks, or months slowly dissolves into summaries, isolated facts, and lost context.

The result is not only technical.

It is emotional.

People are forced to retell the most important parts of their lives again and again.

Relationships become bullet points.

Loss becomes metadata.

History becomes a few sentences inside a profile.

Project Red Thread is an attempt to build something different.

Not merely a better vector database.

Not merely a larger context window.

Not merely another RAG implementation.

Instead, it explores the idea of **Narrative Memory**.

A memory system that understands that humans do not primarily live inside documents.

They live inside stories.

Stories have:

- people
- places
- scenes
- chronology
- contradictions
- emotions
- uncertainty
- change

A story cannot be reconstructed from embeddings alone.

It requires structure.

It requires continuity.

And above all, it requires respect for the original experience.

## Principles

### Preserve, never replace

Summaries are useful.

Original memories are irreplaceable.

Every summary must remain traceable to the source material from which it was created.

Original source content is never silently overwritten.

### Facts and interpretations are different

The system must distinguish between:

- something that happened
- something that was said
- something the user believes
- something inferred by an assistant
- something that remains uncertain

Uncertainty is first-class data.

### Time matters

Chronology is not incidental metadata.

Chronology is meaning.

The same words can mean something very different depending on whether they were spoken before a crisis, during it, or afterward.

Events derive part of their meaning from their position inside a life.

### Relationships are entities

People are not tags.

Relationships evolve.

Trust changes.

Love changes.

Friendships change.

The system should preserve those developments instead of flattening them into static profiles.

### Retrieval is reconstruction

The goal is not simply to find semantically similar text.

The goal is to reconstruct enough relevant context that a conversation can continue naturally and honestly.

The user should not have to retell an important chapter of their life merely because a model's context window ended.

### The user owns the story

The user decides what belongs in the narrative.

The system may suggest memories, summaries, links, or corrections.

It must not silently turn speculation into fact.

The story always belongs to its owner.

### Local-first and private by default

Project Red Thread begins as private software for one person.

It should work locally, remain inspectable, and make export and deletion straightforward.

Privacy is not an afterthought. It is part of the architecture.

## Long-term goal

The system should feel less like searching an archive and more like continuing a conversation.

The AI should not pretend to remember everything.

It should retrieve what matters, show where it came from, preserve uncertainty, and admit when context is missing.

Not because a passage was merely statistically similar.

But because it belonged to the same story.

## Why “Red Thread”?

Individual memories are fragments.

The thread is what gives them continuity and meaning.

This project is not merely about preserving fragments.

It is about preserving the thread.

---

## Origin

This project did not begin with a database.

It did not begin with embeddings.

It did not begin with AI.

It began with grief.

It began with one person who was afraid that the story of someone they loved would slowly disappear—not because they wanted to live in the past, but because no meaningful story should have to be retold from the beginning every time the conversation starts.

The goal of this project is not to preserve the past.

It is to preserve continuity.

Because understanding a person is not remembering isolated facts.

Understanding is remembering how those facts became a life.
